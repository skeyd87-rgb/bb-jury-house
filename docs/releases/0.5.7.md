# 0.5.7

Houseguests do what they say, unless something changes their mind.

An audit over 400 simulated seasons compared what each houseguest would tell you they were going to do with what they then did.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Both nominees were the two the HoH's plan named | 30.5% | 100% (unless the HoH is swayed) |
| A voter who reads as decided votes that way | 98.1% | 98.4% |
| A nomination that hits an ally | 1.9% | 2.0% |
| Promises that bind a houseguest's own behaviour | none possible | recorded and enforced |

- **The HoH makes a plan and keeps it.** Nominations used to be rolled at the ceremony, sampling from the HoH's top four. Anything said before that — to you, or in a huddle you overheard — described their top two, which was right for both nominees less than a third of the time. The roll now happens once, the first time the plan matters, and that is the plan. It changes only if the HoH is genuinely moved: someone they have warmed to or promised safety drops out; someone they have turned sharply against can take the weaker nominee's place.
- **What you overhear is the real plan.** A huddle where the HoH lays out nominations now reports that plan, not an estimate of it.
- **A houseguest's word binds them.** When a houseguest sincerely promises you safety or their vote, it is recorded ("Marcus gave you their word…"), shows in House Read, holds them back in proportion to their loyalty, and is a public betrayal if they break it. A loyal HoH who promised safety kept it in over 90% of test cases; a loyal voter kept a vote promise in over 85%. Before this, nothing a houseguest said tied them to anything.
- **They know their own minds when they talk.** Each houseguest's prompt now states their actual nomination plan and vote, with the instruction that they may hide or lie about it but must not sincerely promise the opposite. If they broke a promise to you, the prompt tells them so and not to deny it.
- **Offline replies answer the two questions that matter.** "Am I safe?" to the HoH and "Do I have your vote?" now get a yes (a recorded promise), a no, or an honest "not decided", from the real decision. They were vague regardless of the truth.

Needs both deploys (the room server shares this logic for online games).

Validation: 9 new consistency regressions; the 400-season audit before and after; 200 simulated seasons without errors; betrayal, grounding, promise, navigation, jury-spectator and 17 multiplayer regressions; production build. Not verified: live AI replies actually using the new promise field — the local environment has no AI connection, so that path is exercised only through the offline writer and by applying the effect directly.
