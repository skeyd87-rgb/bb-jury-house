# 0.6.1

Houseguests keep the deals they make. Fixes from Brittany's first season (exported 2026-11-18).

**Deals about what someone will do to a third person now exist, and are kept.**
- "Put Flynn up as the replacement if I veto Gus" and "use the veto on Gus" are now deals the game understands. Before, they were filed as ordinary safety promises: in her week 1, Rae agreed to name Flynn, named Bev instead, and the record said she had kept her word.
- A houseguest who makes such a deal now acts on it, usually. How likely depends on how loyal they are and how they feel about you by then: Rae (very loyal, trusted you) names the promised replacement about 99% of the time in the tests; someone who has since turned on you, much less.
- A deal is judged where it happens: at the nominations, the veto meeting or the replacement. Kept, the person it was made to warms to the one who kept it; broken, they hold a grudge and the house is told. A deal that hinged on a veto nobody used just lapses.
- A veto holder now weighs what the HoH offered for using it. In week 4 Brittany offered Gus a backdoor on Rae if he used the veto; he never considered it. Now an offer to get rid of someone the holder wants gone makes them much more likely to use it.
- Your own offers work the same way and bind you: "I'll backdoor Rae if you use the veto" is your deal, judged at the replacement.

**Promises are filed under the right person.**
- A houseguest's commitment was sometimes also recorded as one you made, and later marked broken against you. It is now recorded once, as theirs.
- A "promise" that only protected you ("Brittany expects Marcus to protect her") was recorded as a promise from you to yourself. It is no longer recorded.

**The jury.**
- A juror's reasons now have to support their vote. Zoe wrote that Brittany's answer "gave me something concrete … which I respect" and voted for Bev. The fact-check now refuses a reason that argues for the other finalist, and the juror gets one chance to fix it.
- A juror's question can no longer tell a finalist they held a veto they never held ("you chose to let the veto stand", asked of Brittany about week 5, when Rae held it). The record is checked directly, and the juror is asked again with the correct fact.
- When an AI finalist's answer fails, the stand-in answer now owns the real thing that finalist did to that juror ("I put you on the block in week 1…"), instead of the same line for every question.

**The offline writer stops repeating itself.** It no longer gives the same line twice in a row in one conversation (Rae answered "How are you holding up?" three times running), and has a few lines that fit where the houseguest stands that week.

Needs the room server redeployed for online games.

Testing fix: on Windows the multiplayer test left its local server running after every run; enough runs used up the machine's memory and the suite died partway. It now stops the server (and everything it started) and exits cleanly.

Validation: 10 new deal regressions built from her season (the deal recorded once and as the houseguest's; a loyal HoH naming the promised replacement more than 85% of the time against a baseline under 50%; kept and broken judged correctly; a veto-hinged deal lapsing; the self-promise dropped; the player's own offer kept as theirs; the veto offer moving the holder; a holder keeping a veto deal), two new grounding regressions (the false veto premise corrected through one retry; a failed finalist answer still answering the question), and every existing suite: 32 grounding, 13 betrayal, 9 consistency, promise, navigation, jury-spectator, 21 multiplayer; 60 simulated seasons; production build.
Live replay against the real model: 18 lines, 15 as written, 2 after one correction, 1 replaced by the offline writer (a week-label case the fact-check has refused before), 0 flagged; plus a new scenario where the live HoH agreed to a replacement deal and it was recorded as her deal, not the player's. Jury replay, one finale each: no votes fell to the offline formula (0 of 21).

Not verified: a full season played with deals in it; whether the new vote-versus-reasoning rule catches a mismatch with the live model (none occurred in the jury replay, and I did not read every juror's reasoning); the online path for any of this with real players.
