# 0.4.1

## Houseguests no longer invent betrayals

An audit of how promises, alliances and grudges are recorded found seven ways the engine could file a betrayal that never happened. Each is fixed and has a regression test (`scripts/betrayal-regression-tests.mjs`, 13 tests).

- **Naming your target promised them safety.** "You're safe, I'm going after Marcus" recorded a safety promise to Marcus, because any name in the sentence counted. Nominating him then "broke" it. Only a name that is the object of the protection counts now.
- **"This week" promises never expired.** "I won't put you up this week" stood all season; nominating that person a month later was scored as a broken promise. Week-scoped safety promises now settle as kept at the end of the week (and earn a little trust); week-scoped vote promises that never came up are voided.
- **An offer formed an alliance.** When a houseguest proposed working together, the alliance was created without you agreeing, and a later vote against them was a betrayal of it. An alliance now needs your yes; the chat tells you an offer is on the table.
- **A hunch became a grudge.** If the AI merely suspected a lie, a permanent "caught what felt like a lie" grudge was filed with no fact behind it. Suspicion now costs a little trust and nothing else.
- **Forced choices were betrayals.** As HoH with only allies left to nominate, or voting between two allies, you were scored as betraying the alliance. Not any more; a specific promise still binds.
- **Nominating someone broke a promise about your vote.** Each action now only breaks the kinds of promise it can actually break.
- **Mentioning someone made them your target.** "Do you think Marcus will nominate me?" was stored as you pushing Marcus as a target; if it leaked, Marcus held it against you. The offline writer now ignores questions, negations and worries about yourself, and the engine ignores an AI-suggested target unless you said the name.

Also: every houseguest's prompt now carries a ledger from the engine record (promises you broke to them, promises you kept, the weeks you nominated them) and, when all three are clean, an explicit statement that you have not betrayed them. Memory notes are written with your name instead of "you", which the model could read as itself.

Existing saves: grudges and broken promises already recorded stay recorded. The fixes apply from the next thing that happens.

## Performance

Measured on Intel Iris Xe integrated graphics, where 0.4.0 took about 65 ms a frame on High in a small window.

- The static house is merged into a few meshes (692 draw calls to 475 per pass).
- Houseguests cast shadows from a simple proxy instead of their full 55k-triangle bodies.
- Shadow map 4096 back to 2048 and redrawn every other frame.
- 4x MSAA replaced by one FXAA pass; ambient occlusion runs at half resolution.
- Fabric sheen and clearcoat on houseguests are High-only.
- Five quality levels instead of four, each trading effects or render resolution for speed. Integrated GPUs start at Balanced. The game steps down by itself when frames average over 24 ms and remembers the level. **Menu → Graphics** switches between High, Balanced and Fast.

High now measures about 38 ms a frame in the same test. Lower levels measured between 20 and 34 ms across runs; the test pane was in the background, which makes those numbers noisy, so the real frame rate on a visible window is not yet confirmed.

Validation: production build; 200 simulated seasons without errors; 13 betrayal, 28 grounding, multiplayer, jury-spectator, promise and navigation regressions.
