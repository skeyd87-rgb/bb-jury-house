# 0.5.1

## Nothing sits on top of anything else

- **House intel no longer runs under the dock.** Its position assumed the dock was a fixed number of rows; adding House Read made the dock taller and the two overlapped on phones. The gap is now measured, so it holds however many buttons the dock has, in single-player and online.
- **A conversation owns its column.** With a chat open on a wide screen the clock, camera label and room code sat inside the chat header and a notice landed across its title. They now step aside, the notice drops under the slate, and the dock moves out from under the panel.
- **Notices own the top-right corner** while they are up; the clock and room code hide instead of showing through.
- **Menus are solid, stay on screen, and sit on top.** The Menu list could run off the right edge, and text behind an open menu read through it. On phones an open menu replaces the lower third.
- House intel scrolls inside its own box if it is ever taller than the space under the slate.

## Online: people stay where they are

A houseguest played by a connected person now only moves when that person moves. Before, their position was dropped 2.5 seconds after their last step and the AI took over the body — and since positions were only sent while walking, anyone who stood still was seen wandering off on everybody else's screen. Each player also reports their position once a second while standing still, so someone who joins or reconnects sees them in the right place.

## Online: AI houseguests are in the same place for everyone

Each screen used to walk the AI houseguests by itself, so no two players agreed on where anyone was. Now one connected player's game (the one with the lowest seat id; everyone works that out from the same roster) runs their movement and broadcasts it five times a second, and everyone else plays it back. That includes where a huddle forms, so the rings are in the same spot for all. An AI houseguest someone is talking to stops on every screen. If that player leaves, the next one takes over; if updates stop, houseguests carry on walking locally after 2.5 seconds.

Limits: the driving player's tab has to be visible — a browser stops animating a background tab, and the others then fall back to local movement until it returns. Movement is smoothed between updates, so positions agree to within a step, not to the pixel.

**Needs the room server redeployed** (`npx wrangler deploy`): it relays the new messages. Against an older server the game still works, with AI houseguests walking independently as before.

Validation: element-overlap checks at 1280x800, 1024x600, 844x390 and 390x844 with the dock, each menu open, a notice, and a chat with a notice (all clean); a second client joined a local room as Rae — she held position for 17 seconds without input, moved to a reported position, and was handed back to the AI when that client disconnected. With that second client driving, the browser's Marcus and Gus tracked the positions it sent, a batch that tried to move the other human's houseguest was refused, and both resumed local movement when the batches stopped. 13 multiplayer regressions pass, one new. Not checked: two real browsers side by side, and a physical phone.
